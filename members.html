PhD student

Hagar Naim

Co-advisor: Prof. Noga Kronfeld-Schor

Hagar joined the group in May 2021 as a PhD student after completing her M.Sc. in Ecology and Environmental Studies at the Faculty of Life Sciences, Tel Aviv University.

Her PhD research focuses on investigating the effects of light pollution on the immune system, with an emphasis on the intricate interplay between the biological clock and both adaptive and innate immune responses.

To explore the role of the biological clock in regulating immune function, Hagar employs a unique animal model (Acomys spp.) that includes both nocturnal and diurnal species.

PhD student

Yael Shenkerman

Yael joined our group in March 2024 as a PhD student. She previously earned her M.Sc. in Life Sciences from Ben-Gurion University and holds an MBA in Technology Management, Innovation & Entrepreneurship from Tel Aviv University.

Yael’s research focuses on characterizing memory B cell (MBC) subphenotypes generated by vaccination, aiming to understand their distinctions from infection-induced MBCs.

Her project investigates the functional roles of MBC subphenotypes in immune protection and response durability, with the goal of identifying biomarkers predictive of vaccine efficacy. This work could ultimately advance vaccine design by illuminating mechanisms that support long-lasting immune memory and protection.

Meital Mazin

Meital joined our group in October 2022 after completing her B.Sc. in the Interdisciplinary Program in Medical and Life Sciences at Tel Aviv University.

Her M.Sc. research focused on identifying distinct tumor-infiltrating B cell (TIL-B) populations in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) and investigating IgA secretion within the mucosal TNBC microenvironment. This work involved an extensive computational analysis of single-cell sequencing data.

M.Sc. student

Lily Ayala Roth

Co-advisor: Prof. Noga Kronfeld-Schor

Lily joined our group in October 2025 after completing her B.Sc. in Biology and Environmental Sciences at the Faculty of Sciences, the Hebrew University of Jerusalem.

Lily's research is in the field of eco-immunology and focuses on the effects of artificial light at night (ALAN) on the immune system, using diurnal and nocturnal wild spiny mice (Acomys spp.) maintained under semi-natural conditions. Her work examines how ALAN influences daily rhythms in immune function, as well as wound healing dynamics and immune cell populations during tissue regeneration.
